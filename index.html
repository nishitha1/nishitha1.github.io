About

I'm a software engineer at Amazon, where I work on systems that vend promotional content and power the backend for Prime Video Clips, the short-form video feed in the mobile apps.

Four years in, my work has centered on the layer where infrastructure meets something a customer actually sees. I migrated a real-time aggregation pipeline from Amazon Kinesis Data Analytics for SQL Applications to Kinesis Data Analytics for Apache Flink and ran the production cutover with zero data loss. I implemented chaos engineering tests for Prime Video Clips using Gremlin fault injection and JUnit 5, validating steady state under injected latency and wiring the tests into CI/CD as automated approval gates. Earlier, in Retail, I onboarded a containerized computer vision model to SageMaker Batch Transform and built the search and filtering experience on Amazon Paint Finder.

The projects I've enjoyed most are the ones where I had to learn the stack as I went. Flink, Scala, Gremlin, and an internal query language (Datapath) were all new to me when the work called for them.

I build with accessibility as a baseline. At Amazon Retail, that shaped how I built the widgets and filtering experience for the Amazon Paint Finder, Exterior Paint Finder, and Flooring Finder pages, down to catching a text overflow bug in German before it reached customers. I rigorously test my own logic through bug bashes and UAT sessions prior to launch. When production issues arise, I document the fixes so future on-call engineers are set up for success. I’m also passionate about engineering culture, having served as both an official intern mentor and an onboarding buddy for newer developers.

I earned my master's degree from UC Irvine, where I conducted NLP research at the LaLA Lab. My undergraduate research on Visual Gesture Recognition was published at IEEE ICICCS, I continue to explore advancements in machine learning in my personal time.
